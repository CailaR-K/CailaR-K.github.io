I Wet My Plants

Product Design · UX/UI · Front-End Development

Making plant care easier to understand, manage, and maintain for individuals and businesses.

I Wet My Plants is a web application designed to help individuals and businesses manage their plant care needs. Users can create profiles for their plants, organize care requirements, and keep track of tasks such as watering, while accessing information about each plant's specific needs.

The project explored how information architecture, interface design, reminders, and personalized plant information can work together to make plant care easier to understand and manage. Rather than simply reminding users to water their plants, the system was designed to help them understand what their plants need, when they need it, and how to maintain their care routines.

Developed over three months by a five-member team using Scrum, the project progressed from initial ideation and requirements planning through user-centered design, prototyping, development, testing, and final presentation.

Project Report

Milestone 1: Project Ideation

The first milestone focused on brainstorming and evaluating potential project ideas. As a team, we generated concepts based on our interests and project requirements, evaluated their feasibility within the development timeline, and selected a plant-care management system as our final direction.

Milestone 2: Project Definition and Planning

We developed detailed project and product documentation to establish the application's purpose, requirements, stakeholders, and technical direction.

Product Description

Our project is a houseplant management system designed to help users schedule plant watering and other types of care. The application allows users to create profile pages for each plant containing information such as photos, plant names, watering schedules, soil type, humidity requirements, sunlight requirements, and other details.

The application also includes a database with basic information on common plant species and their general care requirements, allowing users to easily fill in plant information and customize it as needed. As a web application, the system can be accessed through desktop or mobile devices.

The system was also designed to send updates to users through their preferred method, such as text or email, and could provide notifications to an outside user, such as a house-sitter responsible for caring for the plants.

Need and Value

Many people find it difficult to remember to care for their plants in a timely manner, particularly when managing multiple plants with different requirements.

The application aims to save users time by helping them identify which plants need attention and when, while reducing the risk of plants being neglected.

Stakeholders

  • Experienced houseplant owners seeking better organization
  • New plant owners learning how to care for different species
  • House-sitters responsible for plant care
  • Team Omicron responsible for maintaining and improving the application

Technology Requirements

  • Python
  • MySQL for data storage

What I Learned

Participating in the I Wet My Plants project provided me with experience in user-centered design, project management, web development, collaboration, time management, and communication.

Through the project, I developed my skills in creating personas, user stories, acceptance tests, wireframes, prototypes, and UX interfaces. I also gained practical experience with Agile methodologies, including Scrum, sprint planning, and retrospectives, as well as front-end and back-end development using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Python, and MySQL.

The project also strengthened my understanding of how design, development, testing, documentation, and collaboration work together throughout the product development process.

What I Would Change

With additional development time, I would conduct more extensive user research and usability testing, incorporate accessibility standards earlier in the design process, and iterate further based on user feedback.

I would also improve the application's performance, scalability, and security while establishing more frequent stakeholder check-ins and clearer prioritization throughout development.
